2026 TMK HDR Symposium

Research, Resilience & What Comes Next

Tuesday, 24 November 2026 Gold Coast campus · Room G42_4.23 · View map

The Higher Degree Research (HDR) Symposium brings together HDR candidates and academic colleagues to exchange ideas, share research experiences and celebrate the achievements of the TMK HDR community. This second edition builds on the inaugural symposium held at the Gold Coast campus in 2025.

Programme

  1. 9:45–10:00 am

    Coffee, Connections and Conversations

    Register, enjoy coffee and light refreshments, and meet fellow HDR candidates and academic colleagues.

  2. 10:00–10:10 am

    Welcome and Opening Remarks

    Associate Professor Sara Thaichon welcomes participants and introduces the symposium theme and programme.

  3. 10:10–10:45 am

    Keynote: Surviving the PhD and Thereafter

    Professor Charles Arcodia shares practical strategies for navigating doctoral study, building confidence as a scholar and preparing for life after graduation.

  4. 10:45 am–11:45 am

    Session 1 – From Potential to Publication: Mastering the Art of the Abstract

    Learn Professor Brent Moyle’s approach to structuring a research abstract, then use it to draft or refine a 150-word abstract based on your own research.

    Chair and presenter · Professor Brent Moyle

    Facilitators · Professor François Carrillat, Associate Professor Sara Thaichon, Dr Yawei Jiang, and Dr Mai Nguyen

    Workshop format and preparation
    1. Abstract-writing formula (10 minutes). Brent introduces the components of an effective research abstract.
    2. Abstract Writing Challenge (40 minutes). Draft or refine your abstract in groups of four to five, with feedback from peers and academic facilitators.
    3. Closing reflections (10 minutes). Review the principles you can use to keep refining your abstract.

    No advance preparation is required.

  5. 11:45 am–12:30 pm

    Lunch and Scholarly Connections

    An informal opportunity to meet fellow researchers and academic facilitators over lunch.

  6. 12:30–1:30 pm

    Session 2 – Behind the Success: Rejection and Resilience in Academia and Beyond

    Panellists share experiences of rejection and professional setbacks, reflect on how they responded and take questions from HDR candidates.

    Chair · Professor Brent Moyle

    Panellists · Professor Kevin Filo, Associate Professor Gavin Northey, Associate Professor Jason Doyle, Dr Mai Nguyen and Dr Lakshi Senevirathna

    Discussion topics

    The panel will discuss setbacks during and after the PhD, managing criticism, maintaining confidence and deciding when to persist or change direction.

  7. 1:30–1:40 pm

    Break

  8. 1:40–2:40 pm

    Session 3 – Beyond the Thesis: Defining Your Research Identity and Voice

    Each panellist will have five minutes to share their perspective on how scholarly identities develop throughout an academic career. You’ll then draft your own scholarly positioning statement with support from academic facilitators.

    Chair · Professor Brent Moyle

    Panellists · Associate Professor Sara Thaichon, Professor Charles Arcodia, Professor François Carrillat, and Dr Yawei Jiang

    Activity, reflection prompts and preparation

    The Scholarly Positioning Challenge (30 minutes). Use these prompts to draft a statement, then share it with your group for feedback.

    1. Define your scholarly purpose. What questions interest you most? What do you want your research to contribute to knowledge, practice or society?
    2. Identify your strengths and positioning. What knowledge, skills, perspectives or experiences do you bring? Which scholarly communities might value your approach?
    3. Construct your scholarly narrative. Bring your research interests, expertise and intended contribution together in a short statement.
    My research focuses on [broader research area or problem]. I am particularly interested in [research questions or perspectives], drawing on [expertise or approaches]. Through my research, I aim to contribute to [knowledge, practice or societal impact].

    Identify one practical action to develop or communicate your research identity, such as pursuing a collaboration, developing expertise or engaging with a scholarly community.

    Closing reflections (10 minutes). Selected participants will share their statements or insights with the room.

    No advance preparation is required. The activity draws on Avery and Greenwald’s personal-branding framework.

  9. 2:40–3:00 pm

    Celebrating Research Excellence: Awards and Closing Remarks

    The symposium concludes with the TMK HDR Awards, celebrating research excellence, engagement and contributions to the HDR community.

    Closing session · Professor Brent Moyle, Associate Professor Sara Thaichon and Valério Neto

    Featured awards
    • Best Research Paper Award. Recognises originality, theoretical contribution, methodological rigour and practical relevance.
    • Research Impact & Engagement Award. Recognises contributions to industry, policy, practice and the wider community.
    • Rising Voice Award. Recognises meaningful contributions to symposium discussions and activities.
